Mission: Impossible (1996)

★ 7.1 1996 1h 50min Hindi and English Action / Adventure / Spy Thriller
Mission: Impossible is a landmark 1996 American spy action-thriller directed by master stylist Brian De Palma. Based on the legendary 1960s television series, the film stars Tom Cruise as Ethan Hunt, an elite agent of the Impossible Missions Force (IMF). During an operation in Prague to intercept a stolen NOC list containing the true identities of undercover operatives, the mission goes disastrously wrong. Almost the entire team is wiped out, and Ethan finds himself framed as the internal mole who betrayed the agency. Comprehensive Overview of Brian De Palma's Espionage Masterpiece

Released in May 1996, Mission: Impossible launched one of the most successful and enduring action franchises in cinematic history. Directed by Brian De Palma—celebrated for his Hitchcockian tension, striking visual compositions, and intricate thriller plotting—the film transformed a classic television property into a high-octane modern blockbuster. Tom Cruise, stepping into both the starring role and his debut feature-producer capacity, brought unparalleled dedication to stunt work and character intensity, establishing Ethan Hunt as an iconic cinematic hero.


The thematic core of the narrative revolves around paranoia, betrayal, mistaken identity, and resourcefulness under pressure. Unlike typical action films that rely purely on brute force, Mission: Impossible relies heavily on espionage tradecraft, elaborate disguises, hacking, psychological mind games, and high-stakes heists. The iconic CIA vault infiltration sequence remains a textbook masterclass in cinematic suspense, influencing countless thrillers that followed.

In-Depth Plot Summary and Narrative Progression

The cinematic narrative begins in Prague, where an IMF team led by Jim Phelps (Jon Voight) is dispatched on a covert operation to intercept a rogue diplomat who has stolen a classified NOC list containing the true identities of all covert agents operating in Eastern Europe. Ethan Hunt (Tom Cruise) acts as the point man, supported by a skilled crew including Claire Phelps (Emmanuelle Béart) and tech specialist Jack Harmon. However, the mission unravels into a bloodbath: one by one, members of the team are assassinated or eliminated in rapid, mysterious succession until only Ethan remains alive.


Ethan meets with CIA director Eugene Kittridge (Henry Czerny) at a local restaurant to debrief. Kittridge reveals a shocking truth: the IMF has suspected an internal mole within their ranks, and all evidence at the Prague crime scene points directly to Ethan as the traitor. Realizing that he has been set up to take the fall for the agency's destruction, Ethan uses an explosive chewing-gum distraction to escape custody into the crowded streets of Prague.


Determined to uncover the real mole—referred to in encrypted communications as "Job"—Ethan reaches out to Claire Phelps, who also survived the ambush. To prove his innocence and retrieve the actual NOC list, Ethan formulates an impossible plan: breaking into the heavily fortified, climate-controlled computer vault at CIA headquarters in Langley, Virginia. He recruits two other disavowed IMF agents: Franz Krieger (Jean Reno), a ruthless helicopter pilot, and Luther Stickell (Ving Rhames), an elite computer hacker.


The Langley break-in sequence is a masterclass in tension. Suspended by wires inches above a highly sensitive pressure- and sound-sensitive floor, Ethan must download the secure database while battling sweat, technical glitches, and a visiting security guard. Despite harrowing near-misses, the team succeeds in stealing the file and escaping to London. However, once in London, Ethan discovers horrifying truths regarding the conspiracy, leading to a breathless, high-speed confrontation aboard the Channel Tunnel train involving a high-speed bullet train and a hijacked helicopter.

Soundtrack and Score Analysis: Danny Elfman's Orchestral Masterpiece

The suspenseful atmosphere of Mission: Impossible was elevated significantly by renowned composer Danny Elfman. Building upon Lalo Schifrin's iconic television theme, Elfman crafted a rich, brass-heavy orchestral score that balanced pulsating espionage tension with emotional resonance. The music anchors every twist and turn, driving the pacing during the Langley break-in and the explosive Channel Tunnel climax.


Production History & Behind-The-Scenes Trivia

An interesting piece of cinema lore is that the legendary CIA vault drop sequence almost failed during filming because Tom Cruise kept tipping forward due to the weight of the coins in his shoes. To balance himself, Cruise stuffed cotton balls and counterweights into his shoes, allowing him to maintain the gravity-defying horizontal hover that became instantly iconic.
